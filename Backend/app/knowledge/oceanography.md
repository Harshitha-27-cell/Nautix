# Oceanography & Physical Parameter Concepts

This document summarizes core oceanographic concepts measured by Argo floats.

## Vertical Layers in the Ocean Water Column

The vertical structure of the ocean is divided into distinct zones based on the rapid change of physical parameters along depth:

### 1. The Thermocline
*   **Definition**: The depth zone where water temperature decreases rapidly with increasing depth.
*   **Scientific Value**: Separates the warm, mixed surface layer from the freezing deep ocean. In tropical regions, the thermocline is permanent and steep, whereas in polar regions it is nearly absent due to cold surface waters.

### 2. The Halocline
*   **Definition**: The depth zone where salinity changes rapidly with depth.
*   **Scientific Value**: Establishes ocean column stratification. High salinity increases density, while freshwater inputs (precipitation/runoffs) decrease surface salinity, creating a barrier layer.

### 3. The Pycnocline
*   **Definition**: The depth zone where water density increases rapidly with depth.
*   **Scientific Value**: Governed jointly by temperature and salinity (cold, salty water is the densest). The pycnocline acts as a strong barrier to vertical mixing.

## CTD Sensor Systems and Salinity Calculations

Argo floats measure ocean parameters using **CTD** sensors:
*   **Conductivity (C)**: Measures the electrical conductivity of water. Since dissolved salts conduct electricity, conductivity acts as the primary proxy for calculating **Salinity**.
*   **Temperature (T)**: Measures the thermal state using high-precision thermistors.
*   **Depth/Pressure (D)**: Measured in decibars (dbar). In seawater, 1 dbar of pressure corresponds roughly to 1 meter of depth (e.g. 1000 dbar is approximately 1000m deep).
